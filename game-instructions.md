# TakeNeuroIQ Player Instruction Manual v1.0

## Welcome to the Arena

TakeNeuroIQ is a cognitive training platform built around fast, focused puzzle sessions. Each mode is designed to challenge a different part of your thinking under light pressure: pattern recognition, sequencing, memory recall, response control, and consistency.

This manual explains how to play the current puzzle families, how scoring works at a high level, and how to read your performance so you can improve with intention instead of guessing.

---

## The Goal of a Session

In every session, your objective is simple:

* solve as many puzzles correctly as possible
* protect your accuracy
* maintain streaks and combos when possible
* keep performing under the session timer

You are not only trying to get a high score. You are also building a stronger performance profile over time.

---

## Before You Start

When you enter the **Play** area, choose a puzzle family. Each family trains a different cognitive lane.

Current families include:

* **Pattern Rush**
* **Sequence Sprint**
* **Grid Recall**

As more puzzle families are added, this manual will grow with them.

# Core Rules That Apply to All Modes

## 1. You are playing against the timer

Each session has a countdown. When the timer expires, the round ends and your results are calculated.

This means speed matters, but reckless speed can damage your score if accuracy falls apart.

## 2. Accuracy matters as much as raw speed

A fast run with too many mistakes will not perform as well as a controlled run with strong decision making.

TakeNeuroIQ rewards players who can balance:

* speed
  n- correctness
* consistency
* pressure handling

## 3. Streaks reward stability

A **streak** is built by getting consecutive answers correct.

The longer your streak, the stronger your run feels and the more momentum you build during a session.

Missing an answer or breaking your rhythm can end that streak.

## 4. Combos reward sustained performance

Some game systems track **combos** separately from basic streaks. Combos are intended to reward continued high-level performance, especially when you keep succeeding under pressure or push beyond expected thresholds.

As combo systems continue to evolve, this manual will be updated with exact behavior.

## 5. Results are more than just a score

At the end of a session, the game looks at more than points alone. Your session performance may also reflect:

* score
* accuracy
* best streak
* puzzles attempted
* puzzles solved correctly
* mode played
* timing and consistency signals
* performance trend indicators in your profile

# Puzzle Family Guide

## Pattern Rush

### What this mode tests

Pattern Rush trains:

* visual pattern recognition
* rule spotting
* quick comparison
* decision speed under pressure

### How it works

You will see a visual pattern with a missing piece or a logical gap. Your job is to identify the correct answer that completes the pattern.

Depending on the puzzle, you may need to notice changes involving:

* shape order
* rotation
* repetition
* alternation
* progression
* visual position

### How to play Pattern Rush well

1. Look for the rule before you click.
2. Check whether the pattern changes across rows, columns, or steps.
3. Avoid impulse answers when two options look similar.
4. Build streaks by trusting clear logic, not guessing.

### Common beginner mistake

Players often pick the first answer that looks visually close instead of identifying the actual transformation rule. Pattern Rush is less about appearance and more about structure.

### Pro tip

If the board feels confusing, reduce it to one question:

**What is changing from one tile to the next?**

Once you know the rule, the correct answer usually becomes much easier to spot.

---

## Sequence Sprint

### What this mode tests

Sequence Sprint trains:

* sequence recognition
* prediction
* mental tracking
* adaptive thinking under speed

### How it works

You are shown a sequence and must determine what comes next, what fits the pattern, or how the sequence should continue.

Some sequences may involve:

* shape changes
* visual order
* directional logic
* increasing complexity
* repeating cycles with variation

### How to play Sequence Sprint well

1. Do not lock onto the first pattern you notice.
2. Check whether the sequence is repeating, shifting, alternating, or expanding.
3. Confirm the logic across multiple steps before answering.
4. Stay calm if the sequence looks unfamiliar at first glance.

### Common beginner mistake

Players often solve only the first half of the sequence in their head and then guess. That works on easy rounds, but harder sequences often hide the real rule later in the chain.

### Pro tip

Read the sequence twice mentally:

* first for the obvious rule
* second for any exception, flip, or secondary pattern

That second pass catches a surprising number of mistakes.

---

## Grid Recall

### What this mode tests

Grid Recall trains:

* short-term visual memory
* spatial recall
* focus under time pressure
* pattern retention

### How it works

A grid pattern is shown briefly. After it disappears, you must choose the correct matching grid from multiple options.

This mode is about remembering both:

* **which spaces were active**
* **where those spaces were located**

### How to play Grid Recall well

1. Do not try to memorize every square individually.
2. Break the pattern into chunks or shapes.
3. Notice anchors such as corners, center clusters, or diagonal lines.
4. After the grid disappears, recall the overall shape first, then the details.

### Common beginner mistake

Trying to remember the board as isolated boxes instead of a single visual shape. Your memory works better when you encode the pattern as a form rather than a list.

### Pro tip

Use mental labels like:

* "top-left L-shape"
* "center cross"
* "bottom-right pair"

That makes the pattern easier to recover under pressure.

# Understanding Score and Performance

## Score

Your score reflects how effectively you solved puzzles during the session. In general, stronger performance comes from a combination of:

* correct answers
* sustained momentum
* efficient solving
* good pressure control

## Accuracy

Accuracy reflects how often you were correct relative to the puzzles you attempted.

High accuracy usually means:

* you understood the puzzle rules
* you avoided rushed guessing
* you maintained control during the timer

Low accuracy usually means one of three things:

* you were moving too fast
* you did not fully understand the pattern type
* you lost rhythm under pressure

## Best Streak

Best streak shows the highest number of correct answers you chained together in a row.

This is one of the clearest signs of stability during a run.

## Puzzles Attempted and Puzzles Correct

These metrics help separate:

* fast but sloppy play
* slower but precise play
* strong balanced play

A strong player is not just answering many puzzles. A strong player is converting attempts into correct answers consistently.

# How to Improve Faster

## Focus on one puzzle family at a time

If you want rapid improvement, do not bounce randomly between modes at first. Spend time learning the logic style of one family, then expand.

## Chase control before speed

Most players improve faster when they first aim for cleaner accuracy. Speed usually rises naturally once the logic becomes familiar.

## Watch for repeated failure patterns

After a session, ask yourself:

* Did I miss because I rushed?
* Did I misunderstand the rule?
* Did I lose focus when the timer got low?
* Did similar answer choices bait me into clicking too early?

That self-review turns every session into training instead of repetition.

## Build consistency, not hero runs

One amazing round is nice. Repeated solid rounds are what matter most for real performance growth.

# Recommended Play Style for New Players

If you are new to TakeNeuroIQ, use this simple approach:

## First 3 sessions

* prioritize understanding over score
* slow down slightly
* learn the structure of each puzzle family

## Sessions 4 to 10

* begin increasing pace
* try to preserve streaks
* compare which mode feels strongest and which feels weakest

## After 10 sessions

* target improvement areas intentionally
* review profile trends
* push for both higher output and cleaner execution

# Mindset Tips

## Do not panic when a puzzle looks unfamiliar

New patterns are part of the training effect. Stay calm and search for structure.

## Do not spam-click

Fast clicking without understanding destroys both score quality and your ability to read your real performance.

## Do not judge yourself by one bad run

TakeNeuroIQ is built around trends. One poor session does not define your ability. The pattern across many sessions matters much more.
